FORECOURT Campaign 05

One story,
three channels

Forecourt Market is a fictional weekend market, invented for this piece and staged in the same invented Hudson as its vendors. One brief, one system, three channels; every unit below is live HTML.

01

The brief

Client

A two-day makers market, October 3 and 4, in the yard at Foundry Works. Forty studios open their doors; three neighbors set up tables. Free to walk in, so the only conversion is attendance.

Story

One line everywhere: one yard, forty makers. The email tells the whole story, the feed breaks it into single posts, and the banners cut it to a headline and a date. No unit contradicts another on a date, an hour, or a name.

Constraint

One brand system has to survive three renderers: a mail client that squares corners and skips webfonts, a feed that bakes everything to pixels, and ad slots down to 300 by 250.

02

The system

Display One yard,
forty makers
Text Lora at weight 400 carries every headline in every channel, centered wherever the slot allows (the wide banner ranges left), never tighter than a leading of 1.06. Inter carries everything else. The three loose bead colors, radish, cornflower, and leaf, are decoration only: they never touch a button, a label, or a line of text. Marigold is the one color allowed to cross over, from bead to button.

03

The send

SubjectOne yard, forty makers
PreheaderForty makers, three neighbors, and a projector night, October 3 and 4 in the yard
Fileemails/market-weekend.html

Desktop · 600px

Mobile · 375px

Hybrid 600 pixel tables with an mso conditional wrapper, no images at all, live text throughout, and dark mode declared twice: once as a prefers-color-scheme query, once through the data-ogsc and data-ogsb hooks for the Outlook rewriter, with every background pin repeated under both hooks. Gmail's dark rewrite and the Word engine's inversion take no instruction from the sender, so the light palette is written as the source they start from. The one deliberate move: the hedge-dark bead flips to oat in both dark routes so it stays visible on the dark ground.

The marigold keeps its system rule here too: the band at the top first, then the one button lower down.
