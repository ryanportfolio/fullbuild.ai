Norfolk, Virginia

Old pipes are Norfolk's whole personality

A city where the houses predate the plumbing codes is a good city for a plumber who likes a puzzle. I'm across the water in Chesapeake, twenty minutes from most of Norfolk on a normal day.

What I see under Norfolk houses

Something like one in seven Norfolk houses went up before 1940, and whole neighborhoods ran their plumbing in materials nobody installs anymore. That's not a scare line. Old pipe fails on a schedule, and knowing the schedule is most of the job.

  • Cast iron drains under the pre-war blocks rust from the inside until the bore narrows to a fraction of itself. The tell is a house where every drain got slow gradually, over years
  • Galvanized supply lines choke the same way on the pressure side. The tell is a shower that loses pressure when someone opens the kitchen tap
  • Clay sewer laterals with root intrusion at every joint, worst on the streets with the best tree canopy. The tell is a main line backup every spring, like an anniversary
  • A high, brackish water table that works on buried pipe from the outside while the inside rusts to meet it

None of this means your house needs a repipe today. It means a camera inspection and an honest read matter more here than almost anywhere, because the difference between "five more years" and "this month" is a real number of dollars.
