Drain cleaning · The house specialty

Cleared, proven with a camera, and tested before I leave

A drain isn't fixed because the water went down once. It's fixed when a full-bore flush runs clean and you've seen the line for yourself. That's the standard every clog gets.

How you know it's time

  • One fixture is slow or stopped: sink, tub, shower or toilet. That's a branch line clog
  • Everything is slow at once, or the lowest drain in the house gurgles when you flush. That's the main line talking
  • Water appears at a floor drain or backs into the tub when the washer drains. Main line, and soon
  • You've cleared the same drain twice this year. Something in the pipe wants a camera look, not a third cabling

What the visit looks like

  1. Find the right access

    Cabling through the wrong opening is how drains get half-cleared. I work from the cleanout or trap that gives the machine a straight run at the blockage.

  2. Clear it and flush it hard

    Cable for the blockage, then a full-bore water test. If it doesn't take everything the fixture can throw at it, I'm not done.

  3. Show you the line

    On main lines the camera goes in after, free. You see roots, bellies or breaks on the screen with me, and you keep the knowledge either way.

Why Hampton Roads drains clog differently

Around here the sewer lateral is often original to the house: clay in the pre-1980 neighborhoods, cast iron under the older city blocks. Clay joints let roots in every spring. Cast iron rusts from the inside until a sixty-year-old pipe has the bore of a garden hose. A cable buys you time in both cases, and the camera tells you honestly how much time. When the answer is "not much," I'll show you, not just tell you.
