Water heaters

Repair when it's worth it, replacement when it's not

A commissioned tech gets paid more to condemn your tank. I don't work on commission, so you get the actual answer: most heaters under eight years old are worth fixing, and most leaking tanks are not.

Repair or replace, honestly

  • Worth repairing: no hot water with a sound tank. Elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves and relief valves are parts, not death sentences. Most are fixed the same visit, from $129
  • Worth a conversation: rumbling, popping, or rusty hot water in a tank 8 to 12 years old. Sometimes a flush and an anode rod buys years. I'll tell you the odds before you spend
  • Not worth repairing: water from the tank body itself. The steel has failed from the inside and no part fixes that. Replacement is the honest answer, and I'll show you the corrosion so you're not taking my word for it

What replacement day looks like

  1. One price, in writing, first

    $1,450 to $2,400 covers the common 40 and 50 gallon swaps: tank, install, code items like the pan, expansion tank and seismic strapping where needed, and hauling the old one away. Your written number comes before the old tank moves.

  2. Swapped and tested by dinner

    Drain, disconnect, set, pipe, fill, purge, light, and a full temperature test. I leave when the hot side runs hot and the fittings stay dry.

  3. Covered for a year

    My labor carries a one-year warranty on top of the tank manufacturer's own. Anything I touched leaks, I come back free.

A Hampton Roads note

Our water is treated hard, and treated water is rough on anode rods and heating elements. If your heater lives in a garage or crawl space, coastal air is quietly working on the fittings too. Around here a tank that gets a five-minute look every couple of years outlives one that doesn't by a wide margin. Ask me to check yours whenever I'm already at the house on another job. That look is free.
